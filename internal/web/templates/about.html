02 / THE METHOD

Activity without the mystery.

The classifier is a set of explicit rules. It measures the concert data provided by the archive. It does not predict success or measure an artist’s current popularity.

(countries × 2) + (dates × 3) + min(10, career years / 5)

Career years = current server year − formation year. Division uses whole numbers.

Emerging0–10
Active11–25
Established26–40
Legendary41+

Try it: 5 countries, 8 concert dates, 30 career years → 10 + 24 + 6 = 40 · Established.

What makes a Rising Star?

The concert rate over the latest two calendar years must exceed the career average by more than 50%. A recent average of 7 concerts per year beats a career average of 4 × 1.5 = 6; an average of 5 does not. Exactly 6 does not qualify either.

We use a rolling two-year window ending today in UTC. Future dates are excluded. Artists with zero career years are not flagged because their career average would involve division by zero.

03 / THE SOURCE

Four sources. One artist file.

Artists

Names, images, members, formation year, and first album.

Locations

Concert places and the unique countries used in scoring.

Dates

All listed concert entries. Each entry counts, including repeated dates.

Relation

The links between locations and concert dates.

All four are linked by artist ID. The archive sometimes has dates without a linked venue; we display them openly. Source spellings are preserved, including names that may look like typos.

Data is retained in memory for five minutes. If the source is unavailable, a clearly marked previous snapshot can be served for up to 24 hours. A cold start without a reachable source displays a retryable error.
